While in New York visiting a client, an acquaintance invited me to a black-tie party. I did not own the clothes, so I ran to Zara for a suit and somehow got talked into an absurdly expensive belt at SuitSupply. Minutes before the event, he texted: he was not coming.

![Black tie, minutes before walking in alone]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/2024-06-15-manuel-nyc-blacktie.jpg)

I went anyway. The party was hosted by what I can only describe as a cult. I was the only non-Cantonese person in one of the most luxurious houses imaginable—grand piano, velvet sofas, crystal, a view of the Brooklyn Bridge. I did not dare eat or drink anything until I watched someone else do it first.

They pointed at the paintings and said they had bought them from the Louvre. They explained that their organization, devoted to metaphysics and philosophy, would improve humanity. I smiled, nodded, and kept my glass untouched a little longer. I believe they launder money.
